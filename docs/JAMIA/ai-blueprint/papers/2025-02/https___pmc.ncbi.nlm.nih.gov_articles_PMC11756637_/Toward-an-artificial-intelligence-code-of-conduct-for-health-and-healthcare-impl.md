## Abstract

### Introduction

The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) has led to significant transformations in health and healthcare. As AI technologies continue to evolve, there is an urgent need to establish a unified framework that guides the design, implementation, and evaluation of AI-driven interventions across individual and population health contexts.

### Approach

In response to this need, the National Academy of Medicine (NAM) has initiated the development of an AI code of conduct (AICC) through its Digital Health Action Collaborative. This code of conduct is grounded in shared principles and commitments, aiming to actualize ethical and effective AI practices within the broader health and healthcare ecosystem. Given its specialized expertise and insight, the biomedical informatics (BMI) community plays a pivotal role in shaping and applying these guidelines.

### Recommendations

We, as members of the AICC Steering Committee and the NAM Digital Health Action Collaborative, urge BMI educators, researchers, and practitioners to engage actively in refining and implementing the AICC. This involvement is critical to ensuring that the code is robust, applicable, and continuously improved to meet the evolving challenges facing health and healthcare.

**Keywords:** artificial intelligence, biomedical informatics, policy

## Introduction

Imagine a scenario in which a faculty member at a leading academic biomedical informatics (BMI) program seeks to pilot an AI (artificial intelligence)-based chatbot designed to help families discern when symptoms of congestive heart failure warrant a hospital visit. Despite promising early in silico results, the Institutional Review Board rejects the study proposal, citing ethical concerns over testing AI as a substitute for physician judgment. This raises a critical question: what guiding principles should inform the ethical design and approval of such AI systems, and how can the healthcare community establish a shared framework to navigate these challenges?

The preceding scenario underscores the pressing need for a comprehensive AI code of conduct (AICC), especially as AI technologies, including commercial generative AI-based platforms, rapidly proliferate. The availability of advanced analytical and generative AI tools offers substantial potential to enhance education, research, clinical practice, population health management, and healthcare operations.[1](#ocae306-B1),[2](#ocae306-B2) However, adopting these technologies necessitates rigorous oversight to ensure they are developed, implemented, and evaluated responsibly.

The BMI community is uniquely positioned to lead in this effort. By leveraging its deep understanding of the computational and sociotechnical dimensions of health information systems, the BMI community can shape the development of AI in ways that maximize benefits while minimizing risks. Furthermore, the community’s experience with data governance, privacy, and ethical standards provides a strong foundation for guiding the responsible use of AI in healthcare.

The National Academy of Medicine (NAM) has recognized the importance of establishing such a framework, leading to the creation of the Healthcare AICC initiative.[3](#ocae306-B3) This initiative aims to develop a guiding framework to ensure that AI algorithms are designed, deployed, and used ethically, reliably, and safely across health and healthcare domains. As a pivotal effort within the NAM Leadership Consortium, the AICC offers a unique opportunity for leaders in BMI to collaborate across disciplines to advance trustworthy AI in healthcare.

## Why a code of conduct?

The term “code of conduct” was intentionally chosen for the NAM AICC initiative, reflecting a belief that shared behavioral expectations are essential in navigating the rapid technological changes in healthcare AI. According to Oxford Reference,[4](#ocae306-B4) a code of conduct is:

> “A statement setting out guidelines regarding the ethical principles and standards of behavior expected of a professional person or (organization).”

Given the multidisciplinary nature of AI in healthcare—spanning research, education, clinical care, human-centered design, ethics, product development, implementation science, finance, and policy—a code of conduct that is both inclusive and adaptable is crucial. Such a framework can foster coordination and collaboration across diverse domains, ensuring that AI technologies evolve in ways that prioritize patient safety, equity, and continuous improvement.

Furthermore, a code of conduct based on higher order, technology-agnostic principles will accommodate the inevitable evolution of AI technologies ([Figure 1](#ocae306-F1)).This flexibility is necessary given the rapid pace of innovation while still requiring rigorous expectations and standards. By focusing on foundational principles rather than prescriptive technical standards, the AICC can support the ongoing advancement of AI in health and healthcare, ensuring that innovation is aligned with the best interests of patients and society.

## Research and practice agenda for the BMI community

Members of the AICC steering committee have identified several key research and operational priorities that align with the BMI community’s strengths and responsibilities. These priorities, organized around 4 critical questions described previously,[5](#ocae306-B5) provide a roadmap for the community’s engagement with the AICC initiative ([Table 1](#ocae306-T1)). Of note, _we believe that the overarching basis for these questions is protecting and advancing human health in a manner that engages humans as partners in the development and use of technology with agency and impact at every stage of the technology lifecycle._

In addition, and providing an essential foundation for the efforts needed to address the preceding critical questions, we should also recognize and elevate the importance of what can be labeled as AI transparency. The concept of AI transparency refers to making the processes, decision-making, and functioning of artificial intelligence systems clear and understandable to all stakeholders. It involves clarifying how AI models work, including the data they use, the algorithms and logic behind the decisions they generate, and the reasons for their outcomes. This clarity helps humans understand the rationale behind AI-driven decisions, promoting accountability, trust, and the ethical usage of AI technologies. Artificial intelligence transparency is particularly important in health and healthcare as it serves to help in identifying and mitigating biases, which is essential to providing fair and equitable care across diverse patient populations. Furthermore, transparency of health and healthcare-focused AI ensures compliance with relevant regulatory standards, promoting the ethical use of AI while also safeguarding patient privacy and safety.

Ultimately, these types of agenda items emphasize a systems-oriented approach to implementing the shared principles of the AICC. By adopting a cyclical, probe-sense-respond methodology, akin to the test-measure-learn or rapid evaluation cycles used in health services research and learning health systems,[13](#ocae306-B13) the BMI community can lead the way in ensuring that AI technologies are both effective and ethically sound.

## Conclusion

The BMI community must play an active role in shaping the future of AI in healthcare. By engaging with initiatives like the NAM AICC, the community can ensure that AI technologies are developed and deployed in ways that enhance health outcomes while upholding the highest ethical and methodological standards. The combination of computational and humanistic approaches, broadly defining the field of BMI, will be critical in realizing the full potential of AI in healthcare.

Therefore, and in response to the preceding opportunities to advance AI research and application, we challenge the BMI community to:

1.  Connect with and meaningfully engage in relevant convening and feedback activities, including those associated with the dissemination of implementation of recommendations generated by the NAM AICC initiative, as well as current and emerging collaboratives like CHAI ([https://www.coalitionforhealthai.org/](https://www.coalitionforhealthai.org/)) which is applying the AICC as they develop their healthcare AI assurance lab national network,[14](#ocae306-B14) and HAIP ([https://healthaipartnership.org/](https://healthaipartnership.org/)) which is using similar approaches to create practical implementation frameworks for clinical AI tools, and efforts associated with professional associations or academic or research consortia working in relevant fields. Of note, and further explored via challenge 7 below, these are examples of a rapidly expanding landscape of such collaboratives, which will require further efforts to align, integrate, and navigate such entities and recognize their respective contributions to the field.
    
2.  Define and propagate a culture of operational safety and continuous learning in the context of health and healthcare-related AI by focusing on fostering shared behaviors, commitments, and outcomes among all stakeholders (per the AICC definitions[3](#ocae306-B3)). This would entail cultivating an environment where safety and ethical considerations are valued and ensuring that the deployment and use of AI tools consistently prioritize patient well-being and trust. Specific actions to achieve this include establishing interdisciplinary convening activities that can develop and promote AI safety protocols, developing regular training and workshops for healthcare professionals to enhance their understanding and responsible use of AI systems, and implementing robust feedback mechanisms that will encourage the reporting of potential risks or adverse events associated with AI deployment and use in healthcare settings.
    
3.  Catalyze a community-wide dialogue concerning both the successes and failures of AI in the clinical domain using an inclusive and transparent approach to communication, ensuring that all stakeholders—regardless of their technical expertise—can engage meaningfully in this discourse. This goal would be enabled via the creation of platforms for open discussion that involve diverse groups, including patients, clinicians, researchers, and community members, allowing them to share insights and experiences with AI technologies in healthcare. Specific actions in this regard could include hosting public forums and town hall meetings where successes and failures of AI applications are discussed in generally accessible terms; developing educational materials that summarize complex findings in a clear and relatable manner; and establishing partnerships with community organizations to disseminate this information broadly. Such efforts should aim to demystify AI, address ethical and safety concerns, and foster trust by demonstrating a commitment to accountability and shared learning in the clinical application of AI.
    
4.  Leverage past community-level experience to address privacy and ethical concerns in the context of health-related AI via a proactive and collaborative approach to developing practical frameworks and policies. Such efforts should involve drawing on lessons learned from prior efforts to balance data accessibility with privacy protection and ensuring that these insights inform forward-looking strategies. Specific actions in this regard could include convening multistakeholder groups that include ethicists, data scientists, healthcare providers, patients, and community representatives to draft policies that address ethical dilemmas and privacy challenges; implementing transparent data governance frameworks that define permissible uses and safeguards for health data in conjunction with AI; and establishing ongoing mechanisms for public engagement, such as citizen advisory panels, to ensure that community values and concerns shape the ethical guidelines for AI innovation. Such measures will not only help mitigate risks but also build trust and accountability, reinforcing the responsible use of health information to advance AI technologies.
    
5.  Advance basic and applied BMI research agendas to achieve the promise of contemporary AI in health and healthcare settings. Such an agenda would ideally include not only methodological advancements and demonstration studies focusing on AI in health and healthcare environments but also:
    
    1.  Establishing a unified framework for health and healthcare AI evaluation and implementation (not dissimilar from the CFIR framework that is frequently employed in the context of implementation of science studies[15](#ocae306-B15)) and
        
    2.  Creating, propagating, and regularly using standard reporting guidelines for health and healthcare-focused AI demonstration studies, such as CONSORT-AI, SPIRIT-AI, and DECIDE-AI.[7–9](#ocae306-B7)
        
6.  Advocate for the adoption and use of knowledge and outcomes resulting from the preceding items by regulatory bodies (such as the US Food and Drug Administration) to align regulatory pathways needed to ensure the safety, efficacy, and comparative effectiveness of AI-driven health and healthcare interventions while ensuring that such approaches do not impede or limit methodological and practical innovation. Such efforts should result in specific, actionable, and practical regulatory frameworks that are compatible with the nature and function of modern health and healthcare AI applications.
    
7.  Facilitate harmonization across and between recommendations, guidelines, frameworks, standards, and best practices being produced and disseminated by convening groups, standard-setting bodies, regulators, and aligned organizations, in order to simplify and clarify how researchers, developers, and consumers of AI technologies targeting the health and healthcare domains can implement and use of such constructs in real-world settings.
    

By addressing these challenges collaboratively, the BMI community can ensure that AI technologies contribute positively to the future of healthcare and advance fundamental BMI theories, methods, and practice.
